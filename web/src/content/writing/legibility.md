title: Legibility
description: Understanding at a glance

Per Google,

> "Legibility is the visual clarity of individual characters, letters, or numbers that makes it easy to distinguish one symbol from another."

As one steeped in a visual rather than oral culture, I naturally cannot help but extend this to every other facet of my life. Legibility is an incredibly useful lens for me. It applies to my work, relationships, and broadly dictates my posture around how I aim to be perceived by other people.

In my opinion, legibility is the most important factor in building good software. What is a good user interface if not legible? This is 95% of what makes a design "good." If the user cannot understand at a glance what is in front of them, then you've already failed. This doesn't mean that your users are dumb. They are busy, distracted people and it is your job to make your interface as clear (or as legible) as possible. Every decision you make about font, hierarchy, color, etc. needs to be made in service of legibility.

This is also true of non-user-facing code. Your backend logic should be legible to the engineers and LLMs that interface with it. This is super basic stuff but your function and variable names should be descriptive. At a glance, I should be able to understand what any piece of code is doing. The pendulum can swing too far (especially with Claude writing the code) and you can end up in the situation where your seven-line function is preceded by a 22 line doc comment. This is bad. You have now made your code less legible.

Spiderman's Uncle Ben and Shakespeare are the two influences you must channel at this point. It is basically free to document every single piece of code _but_ "with great power comes great responsibility" _and_ "brevity is the soul of wit."

Legibility is a powerful tool for achieving your desired effect in many domains.

If you are looking for a job, you should make yourself maximally legible to employers. Say Company A is struggling to scale its infrastructure under a wave of new business. "Hi, I'm Bill. I've scaled infrastructure through a 10x traffic spike, a Black Friday launch, and a migration done under load. I can help you."

If you are trying to find your person and build a relationship, you should make yourself maximally legible to the type of person you want to be with. Broadcast the things about yourself that really make you _you_ and be clear about what you want from your life. The bad fits will filter themselves out automatically. The garden weeds itself.

In the TSA line at the airport? Please make yourself extremely legible. Take off your hat, have your ID ready, maybe pull up your boarding pass, get ready to take your shoes off. The agent will appreciate your effort to expedite their monotonous line processing task.

A lot of this boils down to:

- lead with the most important thing
- trim all of the fat
- be consistent

There is a good book called ["Seeing Like a State"](https://www.amazon.com/Seeing-like-State-Certain-Condition/dp/0300078153) that explores the downsides of flattening complex systems in the attempt to promote legibility. It is extremely hard to predict how complex systems will respond to changing inputs. What happens to a forest when a single species of vegetation is removed or introduced? How does the physical constitution of a person change when a gene that acts in concert with many other genes is mutated in some way?

We do our best to model these sorts of interactions, and I am not one to throw my hands up and say it can't be done. But one must always have extreme humility in the face of complex systems.

Anyway, the point is this: Legibility is an incredibly powerful tool for getting what you want. Aim to make yourself legible while also being careful about imposing legibility onto things that reject it.
